App Review History and Explanation of the August 2026 Submissions

SIX11 Design Studio · prepared 18 September 2026 · supporting document for an App Review Board appeal

This page sets out, in full, what happened with our submissions in August 2026, what we did afterwards, and what Apple's review decisions were. Every date, version number, build number and Apple ID below can be checked against our account in App Store Connect.

Who we are

I am an individual developer. I write, design, localize and publish these apps myself. They are not produced from a template, they are not commissioned from an outside supplier, and they are not copies of other developers' apps. I use several of them personally — the prayer times, the tasbih, the water tracker and the pomodoro timer are apps I use every day, which is why they exist.

Taken together, this work costs me more than it earns. I continue because I care about the apps and their users, not because they are profitable. Being removed from the Apple Developer Program over the way I submitted a batch of updates one night would end years of work, and I am asking for the chance to explain it.

What happened in August

We had completed one shared piece of engineering across our existing apps: an advertising-SDK change (native ad placement, adaptive banners, consent flow, frequency limits). The same change was applied to each app, so the work finished on all of them at roughly the same time.

We then submitted fourteen updates between 19 and 22 August, eight of them inside 96 minutes, using the App Store Connect API as part of our release workflow. Two apps that share a codebase — an Arabic-alphabet learning app and its English-alphabet counterpart — were submitted 30 seconds apart. The release notes on several of them were the same generic sentence.

We accept that this looked wrong, and it was our mistake. Fourteen updates in one night, in that order, with identical release notes and with the same new advertising code in every binary, is exactly the pattern a spam review is designed to catch. What we did not do was try to get around review. Every one of those fourteen submissions was an update to an app that was already live on the App Store, each with its own code, its own content, its own screens and its own users. None of them was a new duplicate app, a re-skin, or a copy of another developer's app.

All fourteen were rejected under Guideline 4.3. After that we stopped submitting in batches. We submitted the apps individually, in most cases one or two per day at most, never two apps sharing a codebase on the same day, each with release notes rewritten for that specific app and a Notes for Review explaining the situation.

What Apple decided

Apple reviewed them individually. Twelve of the fourteen are live on the App Store today. One is in review. One we deliberately did not pursue, for the reason explained further below.

Why the review history matters

We raise the following facts respectfully, and only because the removal notice says that violations remain unresolved after repeated resubmissions.

  • The same binaries were rejected and then approved. Masal Dinle 24.2 build 30 was rejected on 21 August and approved after we submitted the exact same build again with nothing changed. Bimbo, Hintly, Aquam, Pomio, Easy To-Do, 3D Tasbih and Islamic Wallpaper were all approved on their original builds — no new binary was ever uploaded for any of them.
  • One rejection was reversed with no resubmission at all. Namaz Rehberi 7.5 build 14 was rejected on 22 August and went live on 31 August. We did not resubmit it, did not upload a new build and did not change a line of code or a word of metadata.
  • Islamic Wallpaper was approved on a standing submission after we replied. It was rejected a second time on its 2 September submission. We replied in Resolution Center asking which app it was considered to duplicate, and we did not submit it again. The rejection was reversed on that same submission and the version went live on 11 September.
  • The same app was approved two days before it was rejected. Namaz Rehberi 7.4 was submitted on 19 August and approved; version 7.5 of that same app was rejected as a duplicate two days later. ABC Learning 15.1 shows the same pattern: approved and live on 20 August, with 15.3 rejected as a duplicate on 22 August.
  • No app was resubmitted repeatedly after a rejection. Each of these apps was submitted once after its rejection. Islamic Wallpaper, after its second rejection, was not submitted again at all.

We are not claiming that Apple made an error, and we are not asking anyone to re-litigate those decisions. We are saying that the apps themselves were found acceptable when they were reviewed one at a time, which is difficult to reconcile with a finding that we were producing spam or trying to evade review.

The app that is still rejected: Tasbih: Prayer Times & Qibla

This is the one open item on the account, and we want to be completely straightforward about it.

Tasbih: Prayer Times & Qibla (Apple ID 1639099844) is an older app of ours. Later we built a newer, 3D version of the same idea — 3D Tasbih: Prayer Times (Apple ID 1641592564) — and the two apps grew close enough to each other that we had already decided to consolidate them into one product rather than keep maintaining both.

When the August update to the older app was rejected, that decision was already made. So we did not fix and resubmit it, and we did not argue with the rejection. We simply stopped working on that app. The rejected version has been sitting there since, untouched.

Our submission history confirms this: the last submission for that app is 21 August 2026 at 22:56 UTC, and there has been no submission since. The phrase "after multiple resubmissions" cannot apply to this app, because there were no resubmissions at all.

We recognise now that leaving a rejected version in place looked like an unresolved violation being ignored. That was not the intention. If Apple prefers, we will withdraw that version entirely, and we will either merge the older app into the 3D version or remove it from sale. We are happy to follow whichever option Apple considers correct. It is also relevant that the newer 3D version of this app, which shares much of the same code, was reviewed and approved by Apple on 17 September 2026.

On the finding of dishonest or fraudulent activity

We want to address this directly, because it is the part of the notice we find hardest.

  • We have never submitted a build that behaves differently for App Review than for users. No feature is hidden, gated by region, switched on after approval, or configured remotely to change what a reviewer sees.
  • We have never written or solicited a fake review, for our own apps or for anyone else's.
  • We have never copied another developer's app, bought or resold a template, or chosen an app name to trade on someone else's.
  • We have never misrepresented pricing or what an app does. Our apps are free with advertising and, in a few cases, a single optional purchase.
  • When Apple has told us something was wrong, we have fixed it rather than worked around it. When a recent submission was rejected under 5.2.5 because one localized app name contained the word "iPhone", we removed that word from the name and from the keywords in every language before resubmitting.

The one thing we did wrong is the thing we have described above: we submitted too many updates too quickly, from one shared engineering change, with release notes that we had not individualised. That was careless and it created exactly the signal Apple is right to act on. It was not dishonesty.

What we have changed

  • No batch submissions, ever again. At most one or two submissions per day across the whole account.
  • Apps that share a codebase are never submitted on the same day, and never sit in review at the same time.
  • Every app gets release notes and a Notes for Review written for that app. No generic text reused across apps.
  • Any open rejection is resolved with App Review, in Resolution Center, before any related submission is made.
  • A shared engineering change is rolled out to our apps over weeks, not in one night.
  • We have paused submissions entirely while this appeal is pending.

What we are asking

We are asking the App Review Board to reconsider the decision to remove our membership, in light of the review history above and of the fact that the apps involved were individually reviewed and approved by Apple.

We understand why the August submissions looked like spam, we accept responsibility for that pattern, and we have changed how we work so that it cannot happen again. We would very much like to keep developing for this platform and to keep supporting the people who use these apps.
