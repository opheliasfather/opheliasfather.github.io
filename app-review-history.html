App Review History and Explanation of the August 2026 Submissions

SIX11 Design Studio · prepared 18 September 2026 · supporting document for an App Review Board appeal

This page sets out, in full, what happened with my submissions in August 2026, what I did afterwards, and what Apple's review decisions were. Every date, version number, build number and Apple ID below can be checked against my account in App Store Connect.

Who I am

I am an individual developer. I write, design, localize and publish these apps myself. They are not produced from a template, they are not commissioned from an outside supplier, and they are not copies of other developers' apps. I use several of them personally — the prayer times, the tasbih, the water tracker and the pomodoro timer are apps I use every day, which is why they exist.

Taken together, this work costs me more than it earns. I keep doing it because I care about the apps and the people who use them, not because they are profitable. Being removed from the Apple Developer Program because of the way I sent a group of updates one night would end years of work, and I am asking for the chance to explain it.

What happened in August

I had finished one shared piece of engineering across my existing apps: an advertising-SDK change (native ad placement, adaptive banners, consent flow, frequency limits). Because it was one piece of work applied to each app, it finished on all of them at roughly the same time.

I then submitted fourteen updates between 19 and 22 August, eight of them inside 96 minutes, using the App Store Connect API as part of my release workflow. Some went out within seconds of each other. The release notes on several of them were the same generic sentence, which I had not individualised.

I accept that this looked wrong, and it was my mistake. Fourteen updates in one night, submitted minutes apart, with identical release notes and with the same new advertising code in every binary, is exactly the pattern a spam review is designed to catch. What I did not do was try to get around review. Every one of those fourteen submissions was an update to an app that was already live on the App Store, each with its own content, its own screens and its own users. None of them was a new duplicate app, a re-skin, or a copy of another developer's app.

All fourteen were rejected under Guideline 4.3. After that I stopped sending updates out together. I submitted the apps one at a time, each with release notes rewritten for that specific app and a Notes for Review explaining the situation, and I waited for each review to finish.

What Apple decided

Apple reviewed them individually. Twelve of the fourteen are live on the App Store today. One is in review. One I deliberately did not pursue, for the reason explained further below.

Why the review history matters

I raise the following facts respectfully, and only because the removal notice says that violations remain unresolved after repeated resubmissions.

  • The same binaries were rejected and then approved. Masal Dinle 24.2 build 30 was rejected on 21 August and approved after I submitted the exact same build again with nothing changed. Bimbo, Hintly, Aquam, Pomio, Easy To-Do, 3D Tasbih and Islamic Wallpaper were all approved on their original builds — no new binary was ever uploaded for any of them.
  • One rejection was reversed with no resubmission at all. Namaz Rehberi 7.5 build 14 was rejected on 22 August and went live on 31 August. I did not resubmit it, did not upload a new build and did not change a line of code or a word of metadata.
  • Islamic Wallpaper was approved on a standing submission after I replied. It was rejected a second time on its 2 September submission. I replied in Resolution Center asking which app it was considered to duplicate, and I did not submit it again. The rejection was reversed on that same submission and the version went live on 11 September.
  • The same app was approved two days before it was rejected. Namaz Rehberi 7.4 was submitted on 19 August and approved; version 7.5 of that same app was rejected as a duplicate two days later. ABC Learning 15.1 shows the same pattern: approved and live on 20 August, with 15.3 rejected as a duplicate on 22 August.
  • No app was resubmitted repeatedly after a rejection. Each of these apps was submitted once after its rejection. Islamic Wallpaper, after its second rejection, was not submitted again at all.

I am not claiming that Apple made an error, and I am not asking anyone to re-open those decisions. I am saying that the apps themselves were found acceptable when they were reviewed one at a time, which is difficult to reconcile with a finding that I was producing spam or trying to evade review.

The app that is still rejected: Tasbih: Prayer Times & Qibla

This is the one open item on my account, and I want to be completely straightforward about it.

Tasbih: Prayer Times & Qibla (Apple ID 1639099844) is an older app of mine. Later I built a newer 3D version of the same idea — 3D Tasbih: Prayer Times (Apple ID 1641592564) — and I had already decided to bring the two together into one product rather than keep maintaining both.

When the August update to the older app was rejected, that decision was already made. So I did not fix and resubmit it, and I did not argue with the rejection. I simply stopped working on that app. The rejected version has been sitting there since, untouched.

My submission history confirms this: the last submission for that app is 21 August 2026 at 22:56 UTC, and there has been no submission since. The phrase "after multiple resubmissions" cannot apply to this app, because there were no resubmissions at all.

I recognise now that leaving a rejected version in place looked like an unresolved violation being ignored. That was not the intention. If Apple prefers, I will withdraw that version entirely, and I will either fold the older app into the 3D version or remove it from sale. I am happy to follow whichever option Apple considers correct.

On the finding of dishonest or fraudulent activity

I want to address this directly, because it is the part of the notice I find hardest.

  • I have never submitted a build that behaves differently for App Review than for users. No feature is hidden, gated by region, switched on after approval, or configured remotely to change what a reviewer sees.
  • I have never written or solicited a fake review, for my own apps or for anyone else's.
  • I have never copied another developer's app, bought or resold a template, or chosen an app name to trade on someone else's.
  • I have never misrepresented pricing or what an app does. My apps are free with advertising and, in a few cases, a single optional purchase.
  • When Apple has told me something was wrong, I have fixed it rather than worked around it. When a recent submission was rejected under 5.2.5 because one localized app name contained the word "iPhone", I removed that word from the name and from the keywords in every language before resubmitting.

I have never knowingly broken an App Review rule. Where App Review has told me something in one of my apps was wrong, I have corrected the app — I have never tried to work around a rejection, and I have never submitted the same thing again hoping for a different reviewer.

The one thing I did wrong is the thing I have described above: I sent too many updates out at once, from one shared engineering change, with release notes I had not individualised. That was careless, and I understand that it produced exactly the pattern Apple is right to watch for. It was not dishonesty, and it was not an attempt to get anything past review.

How I release updates now

The problem was in how I released, not in what I released, so that is what I have changed.

  • I release one app at a time and let each update finish its own review, instead of completing a shared piece of engineering and sending everything out together.
  • When a change affects several apps, I roll it out gradually over weeks, in the normal course of each app's own release cycle.
  • Every app gets release notes and a Notes for Review written specifically for it. I no longer reuse a generic sentence across apps.
  • If App Review raises anything, I work it through with App Review first and make sure it is properly resolved before I move on.
  • I have paused submissions while this appeal is pending.

What I am asking

I am asking the App Review Board to reconsider the decision to remove my membership, in light of the review history above and of the fact that the apps involved were individually reviewed and approved by Apple.

I understand why the August submissions looked like spam, I accept responsibility for that pattern, and I have changed how I work so that it cannot happen again. I would very much like to keep developing for this platform and to keep supporting the people who use these apps.
